Methodology of the atlas Con el agua de por medio

The rules by which the atlas is built: what enters the corpus, what kinds of links it records, how each claim is verified, and what it still lacks. Publishing the method is part of the method.

What this document declares

The atlas is a research instrument, not a visualization. This page fixes its construction rules so that any reader can audit it: follow a current on the map down to the source that sustains it, or down to the honest mark that the source has not yet been verified. The numbers here describe the corpus as of July 2026 and will change with each version; the rule is that this page changes with them.

The unit of analysis

The unit of analysis is neither the country nor the author: it is the phenomenon that runs through the basin of the Greater Caribbean. The corpus recognizes ten: coloniality, post-plantation, racial capitalism, climate catastrophes, sovereignties, violences, memory and spirituality, marronage, creolization, and body, gender and erotics. A work enters the atlas when it works one of those phenomena in depth from the Caribbean, not when it merely mentions it. The place that hosts the work is recorded, but stays in the background: Cartagena is better understood alongside New Orleans and Paramaribo than alongside Bogotá.

The admission criterion is the academic register or documentary density of the work, not its fame. The rule also excludes: in July 2026 a general-history school textbook of the Caribbean was turned away because its instructional register does not converse with the rest of the corpus. Decisions of that kind are made work by work and left on record, because a corpus is also defined by what it does not admit.

The two layers

The corpus distinguishes two layers: research works and cultural manifestations. The distinction does not separate theory from ethnography. All anthropological work implies ethnography, and a good part of the corpus consists of ethnographies in the full sense. The manifestations layer records something else: living expressions of the Caribbean (carnival, vodou, the sound system, the palenque) as entries of their own, with their node and their relations, instead of existing only as objects mentioned inside the books that study them. As of July 2026 that layer holds six entries against 128 works; it is the project’s declared growth front.

Relation types

The atlas’s three relation markers (resonance, dissonance and epistemic friction) are taken directly from Michael Monahan, Creolizing Practices of Freedom: Recognition and Dissonance (Rowman & Littlefield, 2023). Monahan thinks creolization as recognition and dissonance at once: not a fusion that resolves difference, but a process in which agreement and disagreement coexist and need each other. The atlas inherits that conceptual structure rather than inventing its own.

Two works can be linked in two ways. In resonance, one continues, amplifies or inherits the other; in dissonance, one strains, criticizes or contradicts the other. Caribbean thought is made of echo and of friction alike, and the atlas tells the two apart in the color of its currents.

A third marker is entered by no one: epistemic friction. The atlas engine flags it when a relation crosses distinct linguistic traditions (Anglophone, Francophone, Hispanic, Dutch), because that crossing, which the academy kept apart, is, for Monahan, where the creolization of thought occurs. It is enough for each work’s tradition to be recorded correctly for the map to mark the rub.

The current corpus records 225 resonances and 13 dissonances. That imbalance is declared as a bias: a field this given to polemic does not produce thirteen disagreements in 238 links. The editorial task under way is to reread already-connected pairs looking for the real tension, not just the inheritance.

Source: Monahan, M. J. (2023). Creolizing Practices of Freedom: Recognition and Dissonance. Rowman & Littlefield. The work has its own entry in the atlas corpus: see the «Monahan» entry →

The corroboration rule

Every relation carries a source field, visible on the work’s card. A relation is only marked as corroborated when the connection has been verified against the full digital text of the work, searching for the actual citation, mention or discussion; never by title, by memory, or by intuited thematic affinity. Where that verification has not been done, the field reads «Por corroborar» (to be corroborated), and it is published that way. We prefer the marked doubt to the invented source.

Of the current 238 relations, 132 are corroborated and 106 remain pending. The atlas publishes that state in every version, because declared debt is information, not shame.

The pending percentage rises with each new seeding of works and falls with verification sessions. Both are fine: it means the corpus grows faster than it is audited, and that the audit is never skipped.

Workflow and roles

Every work travels the same road before appearing on the map. The book is obtained in digital form and processed in an analysis notebook under a set protocol that extracts the bibliographic metadata, the contribution, the classification by phenomena and the cited connections with page numbers. That record is systematized in a Zotero group library, where each work is a complete bibliographic item with controlled tags and its relations noted on the item itself. An automated compiler then validates every field and every relation against the atlas catalogue: identifiers that exist, valid types, recognized places and phenomena. Nothing malformed reaches the published page.

Academic direction and the editorial decisions on inclusions, exclusions and relation types belong to Roberto Almanza Hernández (Pensamiento Caribe Research Group, Universidad del Magdalena). Bibliographic systematization is carried out by the group’s research assistance team. Technical validation is automatic, and its rules are the ones on this page.

Open data, versions and citation

The complete corpus (works, places, phenomena and relations with their source field) is published as a dataset in JSON format under a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license, deposited in Zenodo with a persistent identifier. The active DOI is 10.5281/zenodo.21483569 and corresponds to the 117-work version; the current corpus will be deposited as a new version and this page will record the change. Each work in the atlas also has its own citable link and a button that copies its citation in APA format.

Declared limitations

  • Dissonances are underrepresented: 13 of 238 relations. The corpus still records inheritance better than disagreement.
  • The manifestations layer holds six entries. The Caribbean’s living expressions still appear more as objects of books than as nodes of their own.
  • Two phenomena remain thin: climate catastrophes (13 works) and violences (18). Targeted seeding continues there.
  • 44.5% of the relations are pending verification against text.
  • The Zenodo deposit is two versions behind the living corpus.

None of these limitations is hidden in the interface: the state of the corpus is published in the atlas itself. This is exactly where a digital humanities project parts ways with a pretty visualization.
