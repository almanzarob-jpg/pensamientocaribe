Columns by the group

Pregón de esquina

Tite Curet Alonso explained the lyric he wrote for Héctor Lavoe this way: everyone buys today's newspaper, nobody buys yesterday's. A column carries that sentence over it from the moment it is published, which is why it has to be written as though it will be read the day after tomorrow.

Ismael Rivera worked as a bricklayer and learned to sonear on the corners of Calle Calma. The corner was not his waiting room: it was his stage. The same happens here. Between 800 and 1,500 words, signed, on what the research leaves behind as it moves along its slow lane.

What plays here

Debates from the field, developments in the Caribbean and the country, a book just finished, whatever fieldwork left spinning in our heads. Nothing that demands eighteen footnotes.

Who soneas

Members of the group, seedbed members, and guest voices. Every column is signed, because whoever soneas answers for what they sing.

In what key

Every claim carries a citable source. A column can be fierce, and sometimes it has to be. What it cannot be is weak.

The columns

From the most recent to the oldest. The section opens in August 2026.

The corner is set up. All that's missing is someone to start the chorus.
